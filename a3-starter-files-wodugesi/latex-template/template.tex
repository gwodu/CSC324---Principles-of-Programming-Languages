\section*{Evaluation Derivation}

For small step semantics, write in the following form, where $t_0$ is the original term,
$t_i$ is the result after the $i$-th step,
and $\textsc{E-Rule}_i$ are the evaluation rules used for each step:

\begin{alignat*}{1}
  & \ t_0 \\
  \to & \ t_1 \tag{\textsc{E-Rule1}} \\
  \to & \ t_2 \tag{\textsc{E-Rule2}} \\
  \to & \ \dots \\
  \to & \ t_n \tag{\textsc{E-Rulen}} \\
\end{alignat*}

For big step semantics, you may use the \lstinline{mathpar} environment with
\lstinline{\inferrule} to create derivation trees.

\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[right=B-App]{
    \inferrule*[right=\dots]{
      \vdots
    }{
      E \vdash t_1 \Downarrow (\lambda x .\ t , E_1)
    }
    \\
    \inferrule*[right=\dots]{
      \vdots
    }{
      E \vdash t_2 \Downarrow v_2
    }
    \\
    \inferrule*[right=\dots]{
      \mathcal{D}
    }{
      E_1, x \mapsto v_2 \vdash t \Downarrow v
    }
  }{
    E \vdash t_1 \ t_2 \Downarrow v
  }
\end{mathpar}

When the derivation is too large, you may use a symbol (such as $\mathcal{D}$)
to denote a subderivation and show it elsewhere.

\section*{Typing Derivation}

Typing derivations should be formatted similar to big-step semantics derivations:

\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[right=T-Abs]{
    \Gamma, x : T_1 \vdash t : T_2
  }{
    \Gamma \vdash \lambda x : T_1 .\ t : T_1 \to T_2
  }
\end{mathpar}
